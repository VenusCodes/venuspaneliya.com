A few months into a contract, the client who brought me in wrote a recommendation on LinkedIn.

Unsolicited. I didn’t ask for it.

That recommendation said three things that stuck with me.

- “Minimal technical supervision.”

This mattered because the founder had been burned before. Previous developers needed constant direction. Every feature required a detailed brief, a review cycle, and multiple rounds of feedback before anything shipped. The founder was spending more time managing the developer than running the business.

- “Speaking directly with our product team.”

This mattered because most freelance developers operate through a layer of project management. The founder talks to a PM. The PM talks to the developer. Context gets lost in translation.

I joined the product team directly. When a question came up, I asked the person who had the answer.

No intermediary. No game of telephone.

- “Clear, regular, and fast communication.”

This mattered because the biggest complaint founders have about developers isn’t technical skill. It’s silence.

You send a message on Monday and hear nothing until Thursday. You ask for a status update and get a vague “working on it.”

This client got daily updates. Short. Specific. No fluff.

Here’s what I learned from this.

Founders don’t leave recommendations because the code was good. They leave recommendations because the experience was good.

Because they didn’t have to think about the technical side. Because they could focus on their business knowing the product was being handled.

Technical skill is the minimum requirement.

The thing that actually earns trust is ownership.
