5 products I shipped in the last 12 months.

Not a portfolio.
Not a resume.

Each one taught me something different about building for founders.

Product 1: A creator platform processing $10K+ per month in payouts for 200+ creators.

Lesson: Payments aren't a feature. They're infrastructure.

We moved payout state from Stripe's API into our own database.

- Zero missed payouts.
- Founder stopped worrying about creator payments.
- Community grew to 1,000+ members.

Product 2: An InsurTech quote engine where quotes took over 10 minutes.

Lesson: Slow isn't a server problem. It's an architecture problem.

- Added a caching layer.
- Switched API calls from sequential to parallel.
- Quote delivery dropped below 2 minutes.
- Support calls about speed nearly disappeared.

Product 3: An AI form builder for a multi-tenant SaaS platform.

Lesson: AI features without cost controls are a ticking time bomb.

- Added server-side token validation.
- Introduced workspace-level rate limiting.
- Manual editing dropped 60%.
- OpenAI costs stayed predictable.

Product 4: An immigration case management platform handling hundreds of active cases.

Lesson: The best feature is the one that eliminates the support ticket.

We surfaced the information users kept calling about.

- Support queries dropped 40%.
- No chatbot required.

Product 5: An EdTech platform built on a non-profit budget.

Lesson: Constraints force better decisions.

- Serverless architecture reduced infrastructure costs by 60-70%.
- Prioritized 3 launch features instead of 10.
- Shipped on time, under budget, with a foundation built to scale.

Different industries.
Different budgets.
Different challenges.

The pattern was the same every time.

- One developer.
- Full ownership.
- Production-ready software.
- Founder stays focused on growing the business.
