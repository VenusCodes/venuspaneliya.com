A founder asked me last week: “Should I hire a developer or use AI to build my product?”

My answer: both. But not the way you think.

Using AI tools to build your product is smart. They’re fast. They’re cheap. And for many use cases, the output is genuinely good.

But here’s what I keep seeing.

Founders use AI to build Version 1. It works. They launch. Users sign up. Revenue starts coming in.

Then they need Version 2. More features. Better performance. Security improvements. Compliance requirements from a new enterprise customer.

And they hit a wall.

The AI-generated code wasn’t structured for extension. Adding Feature B requires understanding how Feature A was built. But the founder didn’t build Feature A. The AI did. And the AI doesn’t remember why it made the choices it made.

There’s no documentation. There’s no architectural reasoning. There’s just code that works until it doesn’t.

This is where the hybrid model makes sense.

Use AI tools for the initial build. Move fast. Validate the idea. Get to market.

Then bring in a developer who can do 3 things.

First, review what the AI built. Identify the gaps in security, error handling, and architecture that AI tools consistently miss.

Second, restructure the foundation. Make the codebase ready for the next 12 months of features, not just the next feature.

Third, own the ongoing development. Someone who understands the full system, makes architecture decisions, and ships production-ready code without needing constant direction.

AI tools are the best first developer you’ll ever have. But they’re not the last developer you’ll need.

The founders who win are the ones who know when to switch from “AI building” to “human owning.”

What’s your approach? Are you building with AI tools, with a developer, or both?
